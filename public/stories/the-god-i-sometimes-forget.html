The God I Sometimes Forget

Silas Bambil · November 2024

For several days, I kept encountering the word faith. Initially, I thought little of it, dismissing it as a coincidence. But as the word continued appearing, I began to wonder whether there was something about faith that I needed to understand better. One morning, after completing my usual routine, I lay down to rest and found myself thinking about it again. I felt that God might be trying to draw my attention to something. What I found particularly beautiful about that morning was that an ordinary experience involving two of my friends would remind me of something about God that I should never have forgotten.

I picked up my phone and searched for the word faith. Among the things I encountered was Hebrews 11:1, which reads, “Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen” (NRSV). I found myself returning to those last few words: the conviction of things not seen. I wondered how often I genuinely trusted God without first seeing something that reassured me. Did I always need some indication that things were going well before I could be at peace? As I thought about this, I remembered an experience with Ernest and Henry.

Ernest and Henry are two friends whose dependability I have come to appreciate enormously. On one occasion, we had approximately twenty minutes remaining before an assignment submission deadline, and the assignment had not yet been completed. Interestingly, I was not particularly worried. Twenty minutes was hardly a comfortable amount of time, but I trusted those two to get the work done. And they did. What struck me as I remembered that experience was how calm I had been before the assignment was completed. My confidence came from knowing the people involved rather than knowing exactly how they would manage to finish it.

I did not need Ernest and Henry to show me a completed assignment before I could believe that they would meet the deadline. I knew them, and that was enough. As I thought about this, a rather uncomfortable question occurred to me. If I could have that much confidence in two human beings, knowing perfectly well that they had limitations, how much more should I trust God? Here was a God in whose power, goodness, and faithfulness I wholeheartedly believed, yet there were occasions when I struggled to give Him the same confidence I so readily placed in my friends.

I love God, and I believe that He loves me. I believe that He knows me far better than I know myself and understands things about my life that I may never fully comprehend. Yet sometimes, I find myself worrying about circumstances as though He were entirely unaware of them. I become so occupied with what might happen that I seem to forget who He is. It is humbling to admit this, particularly when I think about how much His faithfulness means to me. I wish the confidence with which I speak about God were always reflected in the way I respond to uncertainty.

I also thought about Jesus' teaching in Matthew 17:20 (NIV), where He speaks of having “faith as small as a mustard seed” and moving mountains. There is something particularly beautiful about His choice of a mustard seed. Of all the things He could have used to illustrate faith, He chose something remarkably small. I find comfort in that, especially when I consider how much my own faith still needs to grow. I do not understand this passage as a promise that everything will happen as I desire. Rather, it encourages me to place my trust in God, even when that trust is something I am still learning to develop.

One of the things I sometimes do is limit God in my mind. I think through a difficult situation, consider the possibilities available to me, and become discouraged when I cannot imagine how things might work out. How easily I forget that the God I believe in is not limited by the things I can understand. There is something wonderfully reassuring about knowing that He sees what I cannot, knows what I do not, and loves me through circumstances I may struggle to understand. My inability to see a way forward does not mean that He cannot see one.

I have always appreciated the importance of working hard, preparing carefully, and making sensible decisions. I enjoy thinking things through, and I am unlikely to lose that part of my personality. But sometimes, I think I take on more worry than I need to because I feel compelled to find an answer to everything. There are things I can do and things that are simply beyond my ability. I am learning that I can give my best to the former without allowing the latter to consume me. How comforting it is to know that I do not have to carry every uncertainty as though I were entirely alone.

I also know that trusting God does not mean that everything will happen according to my wishes. I have ambitions, things I hope for, and plans that I would be delighted to see unfold. But I want to trust Him even when things happen differently from how I had imagined. I believe that He is working for my good, although I may not always understand what that means at a particular moment. There is a peace I hope to experience more often in knowing that I can be loved and cared for by God without necessarily understanding everything He is doing.

What I find especially beautiful about that morning is how personal the experience felt. I had spent several days encountering the word faith, and what eventually helped me understand something about my relationship with God was the memory of two friends completing an assignment. I am grateful for Ernest and Henry and for the kind of friendship that has made trusting them so easy. I am also grateful that remembering their dependability made me think about God in this way. I love Him, and I want to become more comfortable entrusting Him with the things that sometimes occupy so much of my heart and mind.

I cannot say that I will never doubt God again or that I will no longer find myself anxious about the future. I know myself well enough to understand that there will be occasions when I need to be reminded of who He is. But I hope I remember those twenty minutes with Ernest and Henry whenever I find myself struggling to trust Him. I did not know exactly how my friends would complete that assignment, yet knowing who they were was enough to keep me calm. I hope, increasingly, that knowing who God is will be enough for me, too.

Silas Bambil
