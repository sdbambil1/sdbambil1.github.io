The Life I No Longer Have to Postpone

Silas Bambil · November 2024

It occurred to me one day that I no longer thought I needed nearly as much money to get married as I once did. Of all the things I expected to change when I moved to America in 2023, my understanding of marriage was certainly not one of them. I had been thinking primarily about my education, career, and the opportunities that might become available to me. Somehow, along the way, I had also begun to reconsider what I wanted from life and how long I believed I needed to wait before pursuing it. Moving to America remains one of the best decisions I have made.

Before moving, I had always imagined that I would need substantial savings before contemplating marriage. There was something reassuring about having enough money set aside to deal with whatever life might bring. I wanted to be able to look after myself and eventually assume responsibility for a family without constantly worrying about money. I understand why I thought that way. When you are uncertain about your ability to earn a decent living in the years ahead, accumulating as much money as possible while you have the opportunity seems perfectly reasonable. My ambitions for my personal life were largely influenced by that uncertainty.

Living in America gradually changed my understanding of financial security. I began to appreciate the difference between having a lot of money and having a reasonable degree of confidence that I could continue earning a living. The latter had not previously given me nearly as much reassurance as the former. America is certainly not without its economic difficulties, and a stable job does not guarantee anyone a comfortable future. Nevertheless, I began to see that with stable employment, sensible financial habits, and adequate savings, I could begin making plans that I had previously associated with a much wealthier version of myself.

That realisation was remarkably liberating. Something I had imagined requiring many additional years of work suddenly seemed much closer. I found myself genuinely looking forward to getting married within the next few years, once I had secured stable employment. The thought made me happy. I could contemplate marriage without immediately becoming preoccupied with how much money I needed to accumulate beforehand. There is something wonderful about discovering that a part of your life you had mentally assigned to the distant future might actually be within reach. I had not abandoned my financial ambitions; I simply no longer felt that my happiness had to wait for all of them.

One of the things I have come to appreciate about living within a system in which I have greater confidence is the freedom it gives me to think about matters other than money. Financial uncertainty can quietly influence some of the most personal decisions we make. Someone may genuinely want to get married or start a family but continually postpone those plans because they are unsure what their circumstances will look like in a few years. What interests me about my own experience is that a change in my environment altered not only what I thought I could accomplish professionally, but also what I believed I could reasonably look forward to personally.

I had previously given surprisingly little thought to how enjoyable it might be to build a life with someone from relatively modest beginnings. Imagine getting married, watching each other's careers develop, celebrating professional accomplishments, gradually improving your home, and eventually being able to afford things that once seemed beyond your means. There would be something particularly satisfying about enjoying those things with someone who remembers when they were merely possibilities. I am certainly not romanticising financial hardship. I would want us to begin with a reasonable degree of stability. Beyond that, however, there is much I would quite happily look forward to accomplishing together.

I rather like the idea of a marriage in which there are still many firsts ahead of us. There would be things we would want to acquire, places we would hope to visit, and ambitions we would encourage each other to pursue. Perhaps something we could only afford to admire one year would become something we could comfortably purchase a few years later. I imagine there would be considerable pleasure in those little developments. Not every improvement in our circumstances would need to be remarkable to anyone else. Sometimes, the satisfaction might simply come from knowing how much something once meant to us and how happy we were to finally have it.

Love is a good thing. I have become increasingly appreciative of that rather simple observation. I have always had things I wanted to accomplish, and I suspect I will continue finding new things to pursue for much of my life. What has changed is how appealing I find the thought of having someone with whom to enjoy all of it. There is something lovely about imagining the happiness of another person becoming part of your own, about looking forward to telling someone good news and being equally excited to hear theirs. I find myself looking forward to these things with an enthusiasm that would probably have surprised me a few years ago.

Given my personality, there will almost certainly always be another qualification to pursue, something interesting to learn, or another professional ambition to consider. I enjoy having things to work towards, and I would not want that part of me to change. However, I have begun to appreciate how much I might miss if I insisted on completing everything before making room for other aspects of my life. Even when I accomplish something particularly meaningful, I tend to find something else that interests me. If I had to wait until I ran out of ambitions before allowing myself to enjoy life, I would probably be waiting for a very long time.

In some respects, I feel like a finished man. This is rather amusing considering how much I still have to accomplish. What gives me that feeling is the clarity I have developed about what I want from life. I want a rewarding career, financial stability, a loving marriage, and a family. I want to continue learning and accomplishing things that matter to me, but I also want to enjoy the ordinary pleasures of having a life outside my professional ambitions. Much of what I want remains ahead of me, yet I derive considerable happiness from being able to imagine that future with greater clarity and considerably less apprehension.

When I moved to America in 2023, I could not have anticipated that some of the things for which I would eventually be most grateful would have so little to do with my education. I am grateful for how differently I have come to think about money, for the confidence I have developed in the future, and for the happiness I now derive from contemplating possibilities that once seemed financially distant. For years, I had imagined building a comfortable life and eventually finding someone with whom to share it. I now find myself rather excited about the possibility of finding someone with whom to build it.

Silas Bambil
