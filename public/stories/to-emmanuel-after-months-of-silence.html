To Emmanuel, After Months of Silence

Silas Bambil · June 2025

Hello Emmanuel,

I have been meaning to write to you for quite some time. On several occasions, I thought about reaching out, but somehow, I always found a reason to postpone it. Initially, I believed I simply needed time to come to terms with what had happened in February. As the months passed, however, the accident was no longer the only thing making it difficult for me to speak to you. I also had to confront the uncomfortable reality that I had allowed so much time to pass without saying anything. A conversation I had intended to have within a week had become something I needed to gather the courage to have.

I think my experience with my previous accident greatly influenced how I responded to what happened to us. I already knew what it meant to have my life interrupted by an accident, to experience considerable physical pain, and to spend months recovering from something that happened in a matter of moments. I had lived through circumstances I never wanted to experience again. Being involved in another accident brought back fears that I had perhaps underestimated. I found myself thinking not only about what had happened in February but also about the possibility of going through everything I had previously endured. Emotionally, I did not handle that experience particularly well.

I must be honest with you. For some time after the accident, I blamed you. I felt that you could have exercised greater care and that perhaps what happened could have been avoided. In my frustration, I felt as though you had nearly killed me. I was angry because I believed my life had unnecessarily been put at risk, particularly after everything I had previously experienced. I did not believe you had deliberately set out to harm me, but that did not immediately make those feelings disappear. I struggled to separate what had actually happened from what I feared might have happened, and much of that frustration became directed towards you.

Initially, I told myself that I needed a little time before speaking to you. One day became two, two days became a week, and when that week came to an end, I still did not feel comfortable enough to reach out. I gave myself another week. Somehow, two weeks became a month, and before I realised it, more than three months had passed. The longer I waited, the more difficult it became to talk to you. At some point, I was no longer postponing the conversation simply because of the accident. I was postponing it because I did not know how to explain why I had taken so long.

One of the things I have come to recognise about myself is that when I experience something emotionally distressing, I tend to withdraw. Sometimes, I isolate myself from the very people with whom I ought to be communicating. I find it difficult to explain what I am feeling when I have not quite made sense of those feelings myself. Unfortunately, the people involved do not necessarily know what is happening within me. They simply experience my absence. What I regard as giving myself time to recover may feel entirely different to someone who suddenly finds that I no longer speak to them.

For much of those three months, I was occupied with my own feelings about what had happened. I thought about my fears, my anger, and the difficulty I was experiencing in coming to terms with the accident. What I did not give nearly enough attention to was the fact that you were on the other side of that silence. I cannot tell you how you felt during those months, nor would I presume to know what you thought of my behaviour. But I can appreciate how confusing or hurtful it might have been to have your cousin suddenly withdraw from you without adequately explaining why. You deserved better communication from me.

I could have told you that I was struggling with what had happened and needed some time to process it. I did not have to pretend that everything was fine or force myself to have a conversation for which I was emotionally unprepared. But I could have said something. Instead, I kept waiting for a day when speaking to you would feel easier. That day never quite arrived. I had given myself time because I thought it would make the situation easier to handle, yet I had allowed that very time to become another obstacle between us.

There came a point when I had to stop blaming both you and myself. I had spent enough time thinking about what you could have done differently and regretting how I had responded. Neither was changing what had happened. More importantly, I began to consider what might become of our relationship if I continued behaving this way. You are my cousin, Emmanuel, and I did not want one accident and my inability to deal with its emotional consequences to become the defining experience of our relationship. You mattered to me before February, and you continued to matter to me throughout those months of silence.

I do not think acknowledging that I could have handled things differently requires me to pretend that I was never frightened, angry, or disappointed. Those feelings were genuine, and I needed time to deal with them. However, I wish I had been more considerate of you in the process. My experience with trauma explains why I withdrew, but I do not want to use it to absolve myself of responsibility for everything that followed. I did not need every difficult feeling to disappear before reaching out to you. I needed to acknowledge how I felt and find the courage to speak to you despite those feelings.

I am not writing this with the expectation that one message will immediately put everything right between us. Neither am I writing to defend my behaviour or to tell you how you ought to feel about what happened. I simply believe that you deserved to hear from me. I hope you understand that my silence was never because I had stopped caring about you. I was struggling with something I did not know how to communicate, and I allowed that difficulty to keep me away from someone whose presence in my life mattered to me.

I hope you are doing well, Emmanuel. I am grateful to have you as my cousin, and I wish I had given greater consideration to the importance of our relationship during those months. I cannot recover the time that has passed, but I did not want another week or another month to go by without telling you how much I regretted allowing that distance to develop between us. I cannot promise that I will always know how to handle the things that hurt me, but I hope to become better at communicating them, particularly to the people I love.

I should have handled things differently, Emmanuel. And I am sorry it took me more than three months to tell you something I had intended to say after a week.

With love,

SB

The original message that inspired this open letter was sent to Emmanuel on 7 June 2025.

Silas Bambil
