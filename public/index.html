📄 Manuscripts in preparation

Selecting and Estimating Credit-Risk Model Updates Under Distribution Shift

Silas Bambil and Shudeshna Nandy

Manuscript in preparation, 2026.

Credit-risk models are developed using historical borrower information but applied to populations whose characteristics or regression relationships may have changed. Retaining an existing model may preserve systematic prediction error, whereas unrestricted refitting with limited recent observations may introduce substantial estimation variability.

The study compares retention, recalibration, refitting, and regularized estimation under common labeled-data budgets. Controlled simulations and historical mortgage cohorts distinguish the consequences of selecting an updating strategy from those of estimating its parameters.

Applied project

Selection Regret in Credit-Risk Model Updating Under Distribution Shift: The Role of Recent Labeled Information

Silas Bambil

Independent manuscript in preparation, 2026.

The probability of selecting the best fitted candidate does not fully characterize predictive performance. Incorrect selections may have limited consequences when candidate predictors have similar risks.

This investigation distinguishes candidate-selection accuracy from the predictive regret associated with selecting another fitted candidate. Simulations and historical mortgage data examine how the amount of recent labeled information affects these quantities under distribution shift.

Tail Model Diagnostics Using Expected Shortfall and Bounded Loss Layers with an Application to Fire Losses

Silas Bambil, Shudeshna Nandy, Clement Ampong, Prakash Karki, and Chi-Kuang Yeh

Manuscript in preparation, 2026.

Agreement between empirical and fitted high-loss quantiles does not ensure agreement in expected tail losses. For high-excess insurance applications, model adequacy may depend on expected losses between specified attachment and exhaustion points.

Established stop-loss identities and an Expected Shortfall decomposition connect aggregate tail discrepancies to differences within bounded loss layers. The study develops diagnostic and inferential procedures for assessing where fitted tail models and empirical loss distributions disagree.

Simulation studies and an application to structure-fire damage records from the 2016–2025 National Fire Incident Reporting System releases examine sparse tail observations, influential incidents, and the distinction between discrepancy detection and localization.

Applied analysis · Conference presentation

📦 ridgeShift: Evaluating Ridge-Regression Model Updating and Target-Sample Allocation in R

Silas Bambil, Shudeshna Nandy, and Yasmeen Babagana

Completed manuscript, 2026. Not submitted for publication.

When a regression model is transferred to another population, a limited target sample must support both candidate estimation and model selection. Allocating additional observations to estimation may improve candidate predictors while reducing the information available for selecting among them.

The study examines this allocation problem within ridge-regression model updating. Its framework distinguishes candidate construction from candidate selection and characterizes their contributions to predictive risk through allocation and selection-regret decompositions.

Numerical verification, computational benchmarking, controlled simulations, and an empirical application to Beijing PM2.5 data support the investigation.

📦 ridgeShift

Version: 0.1.0

Authors: Silas Bambil, Shudeshna Nandy, and Yasmeen Babagana.

Availability: Completed and maintained locally; not publicly released.

ridgeShift implements a computational framework for evaluating ridge-regression model updating when limited target observations must be allocated between candidate estimation and validation-based selection.

Principal functions

  • rs_update() — candidate model updating and strategy selection.
  • rs_sensitivity() — adaptation–validation allocation sensitivity.
  • rs_target_risk() — target-population predictive-risk evaluation.
  • rs_compare_risk() — paired predictive-risk comparison.

The numerical implementation uses singular value decomposition (SVD) and reuses matrix factorizations across penalty evaluations. The package supports validation-based model selection, target-sample allocation sensitivity, and paired predictive-risk comparisons. Numerical results were checked against independent reference calculations, and computational benchmarks assessed performance across repeated model fits. Documented functions support reproducible statistical workflows.

The package and associated manuscript are maintained locally. Neither is publicly distributed.

Associated manuscript
